Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse Source

No Motion Blur, Just Hand-Drawn Smears

Instead of the usual photorealistic motion blur you'd get in modern CG, the animation team drew old-school smear frames by hand to sell fast movement, the same trick classic 2D cartoons used decades ago. It keeps every pose looking like a clean illustration rather than a blurry photograph, which is a big part of why the action reads like it's ripped straight off a comic page.

The Avengers Source

The Nuke Countdown Runs in Real Time

A lot of action movies fudge their countdown clocks to stretch out tension, but the nuke timer in the final battle actually tracks pretty closely to the real runtime of that section of the film. It's a tiny bit of restraint that a lot of viewers never clock because they're too busy watching Stark fly it through the portal.

The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim Source

A Frozen Recreation of 300

South of the Shrine to Peryite, a skeleton is found wedged in a narrow mountain pass with a saber cat impaled on a glass sword beside it, restaging the scene from 300 where Leonidas traps and kills a wolf in a narrow pass using a similar technique.

Fallout 3 Source

Bryan Wilks and the TV Dinner Line

During the 'Those!' quest, young Bryan Wilks hides in a Pulowski Preservation shelter and mutters 'Now I know what a TV dinner feels like', a quote lifted straight from Die Hard.

Halo 3 Source

Carvings in the First Level's Trees

Right from the opening level, Sierra 117, Bungie hid a string of small carvings in the jungle's tree trunks, from an initialed love note and a date to a tiny caveman figure and even a whole hidden caveman family. They're easy to run straight past, but they were part of a much longer campaign-wide easter egg hunt players pieced together over the years.

The Super Mario Bros. Movie Source

85 References In One Movie

One outlet actually sat down and counted every single Nintendo reference they could find in the film, landing on a huge 85 separate Easter eggs, which works out to roughly one for every minute of runtime. Safe to say this is a movie built for repeat viewings.

Grand Theft Auto IV Source

Claude's Old Jacket

Want to run around Liberty City dressed as GTA III's silent protagonist? It's possible, but you have to make a specific, fairly cold-blooded choice first. During the mission where you decide whether to kill Playboy X or Dwayne, choosing to let Dwayne do the deed opens up Playboy's now-empty apartment, and tucked away inside is Claude's classic outfit from GTA III, ready for Niko to put on.

The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt Source

Over a Million Lines of Code

Building a world this size took some serious engineering. The finished game runs on more than a million lines of code, and a staggering 30,000 of those lines exist purely to handle dialogue.

The Super Mario Bros. Movie Source

Chris Pratt's Almost-Tony-Soprano Voice

Early in production, Chris Pratt tried out a voice for Mario that leaned much harder into a New Jersey accent. It ended up sounding so close to Tony Soprano that the directors had him dial it back and blend it more with the Italian-American, Brooklyn-flavoured voice that made it into the finished film.

Red Dead Redemption Source

The Woman With a Donkey's Head

Back in the original 2010 game, a rendering glitch could occasionally spawn a woman with what looked exactly like a donkey's head sat on her shoulders. It was never an intended feature, just a broken asset swap, but it became one of those glitches players actively went hunting for instead of reporting.
