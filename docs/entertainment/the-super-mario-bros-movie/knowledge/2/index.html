Down to Level 1-2

When the brothers head into Brooklyn's sewers to fix a burst pipe, the music playing underneath is a callback to World 1-2 from the original Super Mario Bros, the very first underground level in the series. There's even a sign behind Luigi labelling the area Level 1-2, just to make sure nobody missed it.

The Super Mario Bros. Movie Source

Royal Wedding Guest List

Bowser's wedding is packed with familiar faces if you look past the main cast. King Boo from Luigi's Mansion, King Bob-omb from Super Mario 64, and Petey Piranha from Super Mario Sunshine all show up in the crowd, three villains who probably shouldn't be on speaking terms with each other.

The Super Mario Bros. Movie Source

Jack Black's Chart-Topping Bowser Ballad

The directors decided it wasn't enough for Bowser to just tell Peach he loved her, he needed a whole song about it. Jack Black co-wrote and performed 'Peaches' for the film, and the track actually charted on the Billboard Hot 100, giving Black his first solo appearance on the chart after decades in music.

The Super Mario Bros. Movie Source

Foreman Spike's Grudge

Mario and Luigi's old boss Spike isn't just a random side character, he's pulled straight from Wrecking Crew, a fairly obscure 1985 Famicom title. In the movie he gets a dig in at Mario, calling him a joke, clearly still bitter about being his old employer.

The Super Mario Bros. Movie Source

Punch-Out Pizzeria

Mario and Luigi watch their own cringey ad back in a place called Punch-Out Pizzeria, complete with newspaper clippings of Little Mac's boxing exploits pinned up on the walls. It's a nice little shoutout to Nintendo's Punch-Out!! series tucked into an otherwise unremarkable diner scene.

The Super Mario Bros. Movie Source

World 1-1 Comes To Brooklyn

Before the brothers get sucked into the warp pipe, they sprint across a Brooklyn construction site to make it to a job on time. Look at the layout closely and it's basically a live-action recreation of World 1-1 from the original Super Mario Bros, right down to Mario sliding down a sign post like it's the end-of-level flagpole.

The Super Mario Bros. Movie Source

The Koopalings' Rock Band

During Bowser's rock concert scene, keep an eye on the band, because some of the Koopalings are up there performing. It's a nice touch given that in the English localisation of the games, several Koopalings are actually named after real musicians, including Iggy after Iggy Pop and Ludwig after Beethoven.

The Super Mario Bros. Movie Source

Mario's GameCube Ringtone

Keep your ears open during the plumbing commercial scene, because when Mario's phone goes off, the ringtone is the Nintendo GameCube startup chime. It's a blink-and-you'll-miss-it audio gag that only really lands if you grew up with the console.

The Super Mario Bros. Movie Source

Donkey Kong's First Redesign Since '94

The version of Donkey Kong you see swinging around in the movie is his first real design update since 1994. The filmmakers blended his classic, chunkier 1981 arcade look with the more modern proportions he's had since Donkey Kong Country to land somewhere in between.

The Super Mario Bros. Movie Source

Gusty Garden Galaxy Plays The Movie Out

Stick around for the credits and you'll hear the Gusty Garden Galaxy theme from Super Mario Galaxy playing over the top, widely considered one of the best pieces of music Nintendo has ever put in a Mario game, so it's a genuinely lovely way to send the audience off.
